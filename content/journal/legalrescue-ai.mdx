Two months in, the prototype was a Notion doc and a Loom. Six months later, we were running classification on real customer audio at sub-2s P95 latency. This is what changed in between.

## The shape of the problem

Walk-in clients calling a legal aid line don't fill out intake forms — they tell their story. Sometimes for forty minutes. The product turns that voice memo into a structured case record a paralegal can act on in under two minutes.

- Whisper-large-v3 for STT, with a fallback to v2 on cold-start
- GPT-4.1-mini for classification + summarization (price/perf sweet spot)
- FastAPI on AWS EKS, Celery + Redis for async workflows, Postgres for session state
- A custom prompt eval harness to keep regressions out of prod

<Callout color="coral">The hardest part wasn't the LLM. It was the orchestration around it — making async workflows feel synchronous to the end user.</Callout>

## What I'd do differently

Build the eval harness first. We bolted ours on three months in and immediately wished we'd had it from week one — it would have caught two prompt regressions that shipped to prod.

Pick a vector DB on day one, not day ninety. We bounced between three before settling, and each migration cost a sprint.

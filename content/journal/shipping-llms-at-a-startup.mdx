Shipping LLMs in research is one thing. Shipping them at a startup, where the wrong answer costs the company a customer, is different.

## Things I underestimated

- Eval is the hardest part. Not the model.
- Latency budgets get eaten by retrieval, not inference.
- Customers will paste the entire 40-page PDF into a 'short question' field.
- Streaming responses change the UX more than any prompt tweak.

> The model is 5% of the work. The other 95% is everything around it.

## Things I'd do again

Async-first architecture. Celery + Redis + Postgres for state. A boring stack around a non-boring model. The boring parts are what made the product reliable.

Placeholder paragraph. The number does not need to be good. It needs to be

## Placeholder — latency is a retrieval problem

Placeholder paragraph. Inference was never the bottleneck. Retrieval was, and
retrieval is where the easy wins live: cache the embeddings, cap the candidate
set, stop re-ranking things nobody will read. Each of those is unglamorous and
each of them bought more than switching to a faster model would have.

Placeholder paragraph. Streaming hid the rest. A response that starts in four
hundred milliseconds and finishes in six seconds reads as fast; one that appears
whole after three seconds reads as slow. That is not a trick, exactly, but it is
a reminder that the thing being optimised is a feeling and not a metric.
